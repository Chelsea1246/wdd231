Knowledge across generations

CULTURE IS
A LIVING STORY.

Cultural traditions are not simply objects to look at. They can carry knowledge, identity, creativity and relationships within communities. The examples below are starting points for learning, not a complete picture of any country or people.

Selected cultural expressions

STORIES BEHIND
THE ART.

Explore three examples and follow the source links to learn more.

Ghana / Textile craftsmanship

Kente Weaving

Kente is woven from strips of fabric. Patterns, colour choices and the names associated with designs can communicate identity, social history and meaning. The craft is sustained through knowledge shared between generations of makers.

Read more from UNESCO: Craftsmanship of traditional woven textile Kente ↗ .

Ghana / Visual expression

Adinkra

Adinkra designs appear in visual and textile traditions. Individual motifs can carry names and meanings; understanding a particular design requires attention to its cultural context and use.

Explore an example in the British Museum collection ↗ .

Uganda / Traditional craftsmanship

Barkcloth Making

Barkcloth making is a craft associated with the Baganda people in the Buganda kingdom of southern Uganda. The process uses skilled techniques to transform tree bark into cloth and has cultural significance within the community.

Learn more from UNESCO: Barkcloth making in Uganda ↗ .

Learn with care

RESPECT THE
STORY.

A single tradition does not represent an entire country or continent. Learn from cultural practitioners, acknowledge the communities that sustain traditions, and check reliable sources before sharing information.
